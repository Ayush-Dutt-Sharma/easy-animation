# Timing by reads

Status: draft · Source: ClaudeAnimationBase's guide ("model the viewer"), adapted for voice-led video · Updated: 2026-09-27

You know what happens because you planned it. The viewer sees it once, at full speed, for the first time. Time every moment for what they need to understand.

## Reads

A **read** is one thing the viewer has to understand, for example "the car pings", "the tower is overloaded" or "only 5 workers answer". Every read needs three things before the next one starts:
- time for the eye to find it;
- time to understand it;
- a beat to let it register.

How to time them:
- **One read at a time.** When two things happen at once, the viewer sees only one of them. Put a cause and its reaction in sequence, not on top of each other.
- **Fast actions, slow meanings.** A move can be quick if it's anticipated, but what it means needs a hold.
- **Lead the eye.** Before an important read, get the eye there: a character looks at it, the camera moves to it, or it moves or lights up first.
- **Some reads take longer.** Small, distant, fast or unfamiliar things take longer than big, central, familiar ones.

## With a voice

In our videos the voice sets the length, so the reads have to fit the voice, not the other way round.

- **A read lands on its word.** Its picture change starts 2 frames before the word (rule 1 in [rules.md](rules.md)).
- **If a line's reads don't fit, cut reads,** or give the line more pictures. Never squeeze them.
- **Subtitles are a read too.** When subtitles are burned in, the eye is also reading 1–4 words at the bottom, so the picture should carry fewer, bigger reads.
- **Put holds in the pauses.** A payoff (a stamp, a ✓, a door slam) goes in the gap between sentences, so it doesn't fight the next words.
- **Labels hold still** for at least 0.5 s after they land.

## Starting numbers

These are guesses. The retro ([process/retro.md](../process/retro.md)) replaces them with measurements after the first video.

| Read | Minimum time |
|---|---|
| Big, central and simple (a stamp, a car stopping) | 0.5 s |
| New, small or unfamiliar (a hash ring, a cell covering) | 1.0 s |
| In-scene text | 0.3 s, plus 0.3 s per word |
| Hold after a payoff | 0.4 s |

## In the script

Every shot lists its reads in order, each with a time window, above its beats table (see [the script template](../../videos/_template/script.md)). Then check:
- Does every read get its minimum time?
- Do any two important reads overlap?
- Is the eye already in the right place when each read starts?
